What's wrong with the current sets

  1. 1

    P-01 on its own, and P-01 in the big set

    The problem
    The same point is in two places.
    Why it matters
    You'd prepare two versions of your answer to the most likely question of the day.
    The fix
    Give it one home: set A.
  2. 2

    The big set {P-05, P-18, P-35, P-30, P-40, P-01, P-25}

    The problem
    The points only share an ending ("…that's future work"). Their first sentences differ: P-40 is a mistake in the paper, P-25 needs "what's new" first, and P-18 is a no-difference result the paper already disclosed.
    Why it matters
    In Q&A the first sentence is what counts. A "future work" opening gives a wrong answer to P-40 and dodges the real question in P-25.
    The fix
    Split it across sets A, B, C, E and I.
  3. 3

    P-04 on its own, P-07 with P-13, P-22 on its own

    The problem
    One answer is split across three places.
    Why it matters
    You risk giving three different definitions of one number, to questions a sharp listener will ask back to back.
    The fix
    Put all three in set A, with P-01.
  4. 4

    {P-07, P-13}

    The problem
    P-13's answer is P-05's: every method almost always succeeds, so damage doesn't reach the routing task yet. If you paired them because they share a fix (send real messages along the routes, with hops that can fail), that fix also covers P-01, P-04 and P-05.
    Why it matters
    The grouping rule isn't applied the same way everywhere, so some answers end up in the wrong set.
    The fix
    P-07 to set A, P-13 to set B.
  5. 5

    {P-02, P-41}

    The problem
    It works as two wording fixes, but P-02 is rated Critical (85): episodes are too short for failures, battery drain or the link window to matter.
    Why it matters
    Its follow-up ("so what does your damage model test?") is set B's question, which this pair doesn't prepare you for.
    The fix
    P-02 to set B. P-41 to set G, with P-15.
  6. 6

    {P-28, P-45, P-21, P-23}

    The problem
    P-21 is about how nodes share the channel (slotted ALOHA), not about the livelock.
    Why it matters
    A mixed set makes you reach for the wrong answer. If the set meant "simplifications made for the simulator", then P-35, P-16 and P-43 would belong in it too.
    The fix
    P-21 to set F. Keep the other three together.
  7. 7

    P-06, P-20, P-16 and P-43, each on its own

    The problem
    They're one story: airtime and packet sizes.
    Why it matters
    The numbers have to agree. 4.4% assumes a beacon of about 20 bytes; with the paper's 34-byte header it would be 6.0–6.6%.
    The fix
    Put all four in set F, with P-21.
  8. 8

    P-12 on its own

    The problem
    It's the trap inside "why RL?".
    Why it matters
    The easy excuse, "PPO only sees local information", is exactly what P-12 disproves. It also has to match the livelock answer: no "lower bound", and no "the 8 slots hold PPO back".
    The fix
    Put it in set E.

Things not to say

P-16

Tempting

"The real on-air header is compact."

Say instead

"The 34-byte layout is the simulator's internal record. The over-the-air format still needs to be specified."

Why not: No specification of the real header exists, so you can't back it up.

P-20

Tempting

"India has no duty-cycle limit."

Say instead

"ETSI is a European rule. Either way, airtime at this density is the real limit."

Why not: That hasn't been verified yet.

P-12, P-28, P-45

Tempting

"The 8 slots hold PPO back", or "PPO's result is a lower bound."

Say instead

"The fixed list of 8 was a simplification. Hiding dead neighbours from the agent (masking) is the fix."

Why not: In the tested scenarios the 8 slots always include a best next hop, and the lower bound was never measured.

P-25

Tempting

"Hardware is next." (as the whole answer)

Say instead

"[5] has hardware results and we don't yet. What we add is …"

Why not: It doesn't say what ABOT adds over [5].

P-05

Tempting

"The damage sweep is just a baseline."

Say instead

"Every method almost always succeeds, at every damage level, so we compare route length. A harder version is needed."

Why not: Section II of the paper claims the five-level sweep as a contribution.

P-07

Tempting

"It's a proof of concept; hardware will tell."

Say instead

"Partly, yes: the planner uses that same score. We only claim it changes which routes get picked."

Why not: The problem is the choice of measure, and hardware doesn't change that.

P-18

Tempting

"The gossip arm paid the airtime cost."

Say instead

"Gossip made no difference. Its merge step isn't connected yet."

Why not: No gossip was sent during evaluation.

Any point

Tempting

"That's future work," as a reflex.

Say instead

"The experiment that would answer this is …"

Why not: Say "next" only for experiments you really plan. Several answers in a row that end in "future work" sound like the paper has no results.

Other advice

  1. Say it first. Put one "limitations and next steps" slide in the talk that covers sets A, B and H.

    When you volunteer an admission, it sounds honest, and it's what the reviewers asked for. When it's dragged out of you in Q&A, it sounds like a hole.

  2. For the 7 Critical points (P-01 to P-07), prepare an admission that keeps the narrow claim, not a rebuttal.

    They're rated 75–85 hard to defend. Arguing makes them worse.

  3. Decide what goes on the slides first.

    P-16, P-43, P-21, P-31 and P-52 only come up if that detail is on a slide. There's no slide deck in the prep folder yet.

  4. Prepare the two standard questions as well: "Have you tested it on real hardware? How realistic is it?" (set G) and "Why use RL?" (set E).

    They aren't red-team points, but they're the most likely questions of all.

  5. Learn the 9 opening lines by heart, then one follow-up line for each point.

    9 sentences are easier to recall under pressure than 17 separate explanations.

Good choices to keep

  • All 10 points the triage marked "have an answer ready" are on your list: P-01, P-03, P-04, P-05, P-06, P-07, P-10, P-11, P-22 and P-25.
  • {P-10, P-11} is a real set: both are about how ABOT compares with existing mesh routing.
  • {P-23, P-28, P-45} is the livelock, and those three belong together. Section VI of the paper already says dead neighbours stay addressable, so the honest cause is in print.
  • Moving P-12, P-15 and P-20 up from "watch your wording" is sensible. They're the wording points a listener can most easily turn into a question.
